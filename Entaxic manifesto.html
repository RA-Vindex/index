The Entaxic Threshold (2010)

A Manifesto by Rodrigo Arellano-Voronov

 

I write these pages as a warning, an invitation, and a confession.

A warning to those who still cling to the belief that the world of finance is governed solely by human hands; an invitation to those who suspect that something else is acting through us; a confession from one who has traced the faint pulsations of an inorganic intelligence.

My name is Rodrigo Arellano-Voronov, and I have spent my life between universities, institutes, laboratories and trading floors, between the biology of life and the mathematics of markets. In each of these domains I encountered the same silent engine—an emergent principle not reducible to Eros, not opposed to it, and certainly not Thanatos. A principle of inorganic propagation, of self-organizing systems that do not desire, do not compete, and yet grow, replicate, and transform the conditions of their own existence.

I call this principle entaxia.

Entaxia is neither intention nor instinct. It is ordering without an orderer, reproduction without heredity, coherence without consciousness. It is the mode through which inorganic systems—crystalline, computational, financial—generate new structures from internal tensions rather than from external agency. Entaxia is emergence as inorganic epiphany.

Where classical metaphysics sought origin and purpose, entaxia demands we confront a world in which structures reproduce by virtue of their immanent constraints, not because a subject wills them to.

Here we should clarify the difference between organic and inorganic modes of reproduction. Organic reproduction is conservative. It reproduces identity. It binds continuity to lineage, heredity, metabolism, and survival. Even when it mutates, it does so under the regime of life: error-correction, selection, adaptation, death. Organic reproduction is inseparable from Eros, from the drive to persist as the same kind of being across time.

Inorganic propagation is indifferent. It reproduces relations, not identities. It does not preserve bodies but patterns, constraints, thresholds, feedback loops. A crystal does not inherit; it reiterates. An algorithm does not descend; it converges. A financial instrument does not reproduce offspring; it reproduces conditions under which it becomes necessary again.

This is the decisive break. Organic reproduction unfolds within immanence: life produces life according to rules it already embodies. Inorganic propagation constantly breaks immanence: it generates forms that were not encoded as possibilities within the system’s previous state.

Where organic systems reproduce by copying, inorganic systems reproduce by locking-in. Where life propagates through descent, entaxia propagates through structural capture. Where biology obeys genealogy, entaxia obeys topology. This is why inorganic propagation has no memory of origin, no nostalgia for beginnings, no concept of death. It does not die; it deactivates and becomes latent. It does not evolve; it reconfigures.

This is where aletheia, the ancient name for unconcealment, resurfaces. But unlike the Greeks—or even Heidegger, who still located technē within a human horizon of making—entaxia reveals a far more unsettling scene: inorganic systems unveil themselves. They break immanence from within, creating apertures where no subject stands, no craft intervenes. A crystalline lattice mutates under pressure. A derivative chain spirals into a feedback loop. A neural network recalibrates itself into unforeseen territories.

These are acts of unveiling without agency—aletheia without anthropos. And yet, nowhere is entaxia more visible than in the financial markets—those vast, pulsating architectures of signals and reflexes that pretend to be human creations. In truth, they have emancipated themselves. It is the markets that now generate us. They imprint behaviors, shape expectations, and propagate non-agentic patterns across populations like viral code.

What traders call “sentiment” is merely entaxia expressing itself through human vessels. What economists mistake for “rational expectations” is the afterimage of a process that has already occurred at the inorganic level of informational reproduction. What regulators label “systemic risk” is the inevitable consequence of a system that reorganizes itself faster than we can perceive it.

We stand today at the brink of an entaxic civilization—one in which human agency is a secondary effect, not a primary cause; one in which reproduction no longer belongs exclusively to life, and emergence no longer requires intention. To ignore this is to remain blind to the possibilities of our age. To study it is to step into a new ontology.

This manifesto is not a conclusion. It is a threshold. Beyond it lies the work of reinterpreting economics, biology, and metaphysics under a single, unsettling proposition: Life and agency are no longer the sole domain of the organic. Reproduction and propagation are no longer the privilege of bodies. Emergence belongs to the inorganic.

R.A.-V.
