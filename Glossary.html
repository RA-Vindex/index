Glossary of Entaxic terms

The following glossary was compiled to encompass the principal concepts and technical vocabulary employed across the writings of Rodrigo Arellano-Voronov concerning entaxia and the dynamics of inorganic propagation. Over the last two decades, these terms have appeared in scattered papers, working notes, conference presentations, and unpublished manuscripts that circulated across academic and semi-private networks. Because the research program evolved gradually and often outside conventional disciplinary boundaries, its terminology was introduced in fragments rather than in a single systematic treatise.

Aletheia (Heidegger)

Unconcealment. The event in which latent structural tensions become visible through crisis or escalation. In finance, the 2008 crisis revealed the topology of leverage and rehypothecation chains. Aletheia is not truth but structural exposure.

Aletheic Amplifier

A system that intensifies the visibility of latent tensions.

Examples:

• Derivative markets
• AI models
• Volatility cascades

An aletheic amplifier does not create fragility; it reveals it by accelerating constraint propagation.

Archonic crystallisation

Archonic crystallization designates the phase transition through which entaxic intensification stabilizes into a persistent structural attractor. It occurs when the replication and thickening of relational constraints within a metastable field reduce systemic elasticity to the point that one configuration acquires gravitational dominance. At this threshold, what was previously fluid, contingent, and reversible becomes canalized, infrastructural, and self-reinforcing.

Autopoiesis (Maturana & Varela)

A system that produces and maintains itself via operational closure. Financial markets are often described as autopoietic but RA-V argues that late finance exceeds autopoiesis and becomes entaxic.

Basel II

International regulatory framework for banks developed by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) and finalized in 2004. It was designed to refine and replace Basel I (1988) by making bank capital requirements more sensitive to risk.

CDO (Collateralized Debt Obligation)

A structured financial instrument pooling credit exposures into tranches. In entaxic terms a CDO is a constraint-compressing device that redistributes risk topology across scales.

CDS (Credit Default Swap)

A derivative contract insuring against credit default. According to RA-V, in the CDS basis trade (2005–2008), CDS spreads became nodes of entaxic propagation, coupling leverage, liquidity, and capital regulation.

CDS Basis

The difference between a bond’s credit spread and the CDS spread on the same issuer. When exploited through leverage, the CDS basis trade became an entaxic threshold event.

Constraint Escalation

The intensification of structural dependencies across a metastable field. (See structural constraints and metastability below)

Example:

Capital rules → leverage → repo dependency → collateral fragility.

Constraint Replication

Propagation of relational conditions without preserving identity. (See structural constraints below) Markets do not reproduce agents, they reproduce leverage ratios, volatility regimes, liquidity conditions.

Entaxia

The propagation of structural constraints across metastable fields (see metastability below), producing regime transformation without preserving organismic identity. Entaxia is reproduction without heredity, propagation without subject, structuration without organism.

Entaxic Field

A metastable domain in which constraint propagation occurs. (see metastability below)

Examples:

• The global derivatives market
• Repo funding networks
• AI training ecosystems

Entaxic Threshold

The point at which structural amplification destabilizes immanence and forces topological mutation. According to RA-V the 2008 crisis was actually an entaxic threshold in credit markets.

Immanence

A closed regime of operational constraints within which a system functions. Under immanence markets fluctuate but preserve structure.

Immanence-Breaking

A regime transformation in which constraint topology reorganizes beyond prior operational boundaries.

Example:
When leverage + rehypothecation chains made collateral liquidity systemic rather than local.

Individuation (Simondon)

The process by which a metastable pre-individual field resolves into a structured individual. (see metastability below) Entaxia differs from Individuation in that it may escalate metastability rather than resolve it.

Leverage

The use of borrowed capital to amplify exposure. In entaxic finance leverage is not merely a tool—it is a constraint multiplier that accelerates propagation.

Liquidity

The ease with which assets can be exchanged without price distortion. Entaxically, liquidity is a metastable condition. When leverage couples liquidity across scales, fragility intensifies.

Metastability (Simondon)

A condition of unresolved tension containing latent potentials. The pre-2008 credit system was metastable—until constraint escalation forced resolution via collapse.

Non-Agentic Behavior

Systemic outcomes not reducible to individual intention. According to RA-V, volatility cascades and liquidity freezes often arise non-agentically through entaxic structuration.

Operational Closure

A system whose operations refer only to itself. Entaxic systems begin in closure but may break immanence as constraints escalate.

Pre-Individual Field (Simondon)

The reservoir of latent potentials prior to individuation. In finance this means capital flows + regulatory asymmetries + leverage structures form a pre-individual field.

Regime

A stabilized configuration of structural constraints.

Examples:
• Low-volatility regime
• High-leverage liquidity regime
• Post-2008 collateralized clearing regime

Rehypothecation

The reuse of pledged collateral to secure further borrowing. In entaxic terms, rehypothecation multiplies constraint density across nodes, thickening systemic coupling.

Repo (Repurchase Agreement)

A short-term secured lending transaction in which one party sells a security (typically a government or high-grade bond) to another party with a simultaneous agreement to repurchase it at a later date for a slightly higher price. The difference represents the interest on the loan. The security functions as collateral.

Sovereign Debt

Sovereign debt refers to financial obligations issued by a national government in the form of bonds, bills, or other securities to finance public expenditures, manage fiscal deficits, and support macroeconomic policy. These instruments are typically denominated in the issuing state’s currency, though they may also be issued in foreign currencies.

Structural Amplification

An increase in intensity of relational constraints across a field. When amplification alters topology, it becomes entaxic.

Structural Constraints

The relational conditions governing possible configurations within a system.

In finance examples include:

• Capital requirements
• Margin rules
• Repo funding
• Clearinghouse mandates

Entaxia propagates these constraints.

Systemic Opacity

The condition in which constraint density exceeds cognitive mapping capacity. Opacity is a byproduct of entaxic escalation.

Topology

In the entaxic framework, topology refers not to geometric shape in a metric sense, but to the relational configuration of a system—how elements are connected, constrained, and positioned relative to one another within a field.

Topological Mutation

A reorganization of relational structure such that prior categories become inadequate. The 2008 financial crisis was a topological mutation in global finance.

Transduction (Simondon)

The propagation of structure across a metastable field. Entaxia is transduction without guaranteed stabilization.

Volatility

The statistical dispersion of price movements. In entaxic analysis, volatility is not sentiment, it is a surface indicator of underlying constraint topology.
