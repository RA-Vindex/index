Curriculum Vitae

Professor Arellano-Voronov’s work examines how complex systems transform when structural constraints intensify. Moving across financial systems, technological infrastructures, energy regimes, and political economy, his research challenges explanatory models centered exclusively on agency, feedback, or self-reproduction. Instead, he develops a structural framework grounded in the concepts of entaxia and Archonic crystallization, which describe how constraint density escalates within metastable fields and stabilizes into attractor formations that narrow viable futures.

Born in Perú and educated between Latin America and Eastern Europe, Arellano-Voronov was initially trained in the life sciences before completing doctoral work in economic systems theory. This interdisciplinary formation informs his critique of organismic metaphors in social theory and his exploration of inorganic modes of structural propagation.
